# Operational connectivity decision

Accepted for production: a charge-blind, undirected **metal-center selection hybrid**, with explicit uncertainty. This is a local environment definition, not a universal bond assignment or an oxidation-state model. The proposed mutual-selection hybrid is rejected: it removes whole reviewed coordination shells.

For pairs of nonmetals, accept periodic distances from 0.40 Å through 1.25 times the sum of ASE covalent radii. Metals are exactly `pymatgen.core.Element.is_metal`; metalloids follow that library classification, not an improvised chemical list. For a metal–nonmetal pair, accept the edge if the **metal center** selects the contact using CrystalNN. A nonmetal selection alone cannot create such an edge; the nonmetal cannot veto it. For metal–metal pairs, accept if **either metal center** selects it. Add the explicit reverse `(j,i,-image)` for every accepted edge. Deduplicate only identical indexed-site/image records; keep distinct physical images. No charges or oxidation states are supplied to neighbor selection.

CrystalNN settings: `weighted_cn=False`, `cation_anion=False`, `distance_cutoffs=(0.5,1.0)` Å, `x_diff_weight=0`, `porous_adjustment=True`, `search_cutoff=7.0` Å, `fingerprint_length=None`. The largest coordination-probability set is selected, following `get_nn_info` semantics. Retained edges must have distance >=0.40 Å. CrystalNN's native no-oxidation-state radius fallback is retained and recorded in warning counts; no guessed oxidation states are introduced. Exact versions, module hash and configuration are in `results/connectivity/decision_metadata.json`.

Specifically, bare-element sites make pymatgen `local_env._get_radius` return zero; CrystalNN then uses `_get_default_radius`, which takes `CovalentRadius.radius[element]` and falls back to `Element.atomic_radius` if unavailable. These are CrystalNN's own pymatgen radii. ASE radii govern the nonmetal cutoff and normalized-distance descriptors. Both radius columns and the full cohort's metal classification are exported in `reports/element_classification_and_radii.csv`.

Metal–metal contacts are not universally removed. Selected contacts remain in the graph and trigger an ambiguity flag. Additional metal proximity under the original radius criterion is recorded separately, without calling every such contact a chemical bond. This distinguishes the O8 coordination of the reviewed Zr–oxo cluster from its four Zr neighbors at about 3.535 Å. Potential real metal–C/H contacts also remain when selected and are flagged, including Pt C2/N2 and the Zr C11/P2 environment.

Evidence: all 34 structures/5,198 atoms were compared. Saved 18-seed default/Isayev results were reused, the 16 additions were recomputed, zero-weight CrystalNN was run on all sites, and ten decisive saved site calculations were replayed against the installed versions. Default CrystalNN reproduces the reported 1,179 directed links lacking a reverse. Zero-weight CrystalNN still has 897; zero weight alone is not an undirected chemical graph. The accepted graph has 12,258 directed entries, zero missing reverses, no duplicate physical edges, and four entries representing additional legitimate images of indexed neighbors. All edge distances are reconstructed from lattice/image coordinates.

The strict both-endpoint candidate changes 918 sites and leaves the reviewed Dy, Pb and carbon-rich Zr sites isolated; it reduces Cu O6 to O2 and Zr–oxo O8 to O6. The either-endpoint candidate changes six sites relative to metal-center authority, allowing ligand choices to add metal contacts. The accepted metal-center rule avoids those demonstrated vetoes and makes coordination selection explicit. The contact ledger and complete distance/image rows are in `results/connectivity/contact_review_ledger.csv` and `decisive_contacts.csv`.

Required chemistry is preserved: C44 keeps its short C–C links; H12 keeps O–H 1.0025 Å and excludes the 1.6211 Å secondary H...O contact; Cu2 keeps the longer 2.3985 Å O pair (179.99935 degrees); Dy0 keeps O7/N2; Pb28 keeps O4/N4; Zr–oxo keeps O8; Zr4 retains C11/P2 (two five-membered carbon cycles plus an additional C); Pt61 keeps C2/N2. Longer Cu/Pb coordination is plausible, not a reference truth label. Distant Dy C/H contacts are treated as secondary or unresolved, not evidence that all metal–C/H contacts are spurious.

Bounded perturbations: shifting both CrystalNN distance taper endpoints by -0.10/+0.10 Å changes 8/37 of 5,198 sites (including reciprocal effects). Nonmetal cutoff 1.20 changes zero sites; 1.30 changes 18, including secondary H/O contacts. All decisive metal shells listed above remain unchanged. Exact changed sites are saved, not just change rates. The chosen nonmetal scale is the original definition; the wider scale is not silently adopted. In 2x1x1 supercells of an ordinary Cu environment, the Zr–oxo cluster and carbon-rich Zr, all 1,132 mapped sites reproduce primitive neighbor identities/relative vectors and two-hop path counts. Prior saved periodic checks cover additional orientations.

Flags are conservative diagnostics, not adjudicated error labels: empty neighborhoods, structure-level graph failures, metal–C/H, selected metal–metal, and a small gap (<0.10) between the two largest CrystalNN coordination probabilities. The full table separately records original-cutoff disagreement and radius perturbation sensitivity. A zero-neighbor site remains in the cohort; it may be an ion. Site algorithm failure receives no silent alternate graph: errors are recorded and the whole structure is marked graph-invalid. Invalid structures remain in charge-only analyses. Production failures are checkpointed for retry; they do not change cohort membership. Neither consensus nor stable parameter choices establish bond truth.

Runtime: full zero-weight CrystalNN for 5,198 panel atoms took 41.84 aggregate seconds; metal-only shifted calculations took 1.48 seconds for 190 metal sites. The latter scales arithmetically to about six single-worker minutes for 46,994 cohort metal sites, excluding reconstruction, descriptors and disk I/O. The complete panel took 73.1 wall seconds with two workers and reused seed baselines. This Windows host has eight logical CPUs and about 12.6 GB RAM, with only about 3 GB free during preparation; production therefore uses two workers and one numerical-library thread per worker. Allow approximately 5–20 minutes plus source download/cache, with actual progress measured and resumable. Larger/denser structures can cost more.

Production is justified for broad first-shell chemical comparisons, followed by held-out structure evaluation and an outcome-blind graph sensitivity subset. Claims about exact rare coordination numbers, metal–metal bonding, hydrogen bonding, or hapticity must remain limited. The stored two-hop descriptors count non-backtracking **paths**, not unique graph-distance-two atoms; repeated images and repeated destinations are intentional.
